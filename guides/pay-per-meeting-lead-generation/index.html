Performance-based lead generation guide

When does pay-per-meeting lead generation make sense?

Pay-per-meeting lead generation can align fees with delivery when the buyer, qualification evidence, attendance requirement and dispute process are written before launch. It becomes risky when any booking counts, the provider controls quality definitions, or the client cannot follow up quickly enough to convert valid meetings.

Booked, held and qualified are different products

A booked meeting is a calendar event. A held meeting requires attendance. A qualified held meeting also satisfies agreed company, role, geography, interest and exclusion rules.

Contracts should price the exact product being delivered rather than using appointment, lead and meeting interchangeably.

Decide what resolves a dispute

In this example, the buyer has five business days from receiving the supporting records to raise a dispute. Have both parties agree the actual window in writing. Match the original booking ID, attendance record and the agreed qualification evidence to each invoice line. Name the person who can approve or reject the record. If it was already billed and the written terms call for a credit, tie the credit to that original line. If evidence is still missing, show the disputed item separately and follow the contract before releasing payment.

Resolve the pending line both ways

For M05, the named buyer-side reviewer checks the attendance and role evidence. Bill once if that evidence confirms an agreed role within the five-business-day review window. Exclude it if the role is outside scope or evidence is still missing at the deadline, as this fictional contract provides. Log the decision and issue any required credit against the original invoice line.

Buyer check. Match each invoice line to the original meeting ID, attendance and the agreed qualification evidence. Record a credit against that same line.

Write the acceptance rules first

  • Allowed company profile and exclusions
  • Accepted roles or buying responsibility
  • Required geography and company size
  • Evidence of interest or relevant need
  • Attendance threshold and lateness rule
  • Duplicate and existing-opportunity window
  • Reschedule and no-show treatment
  • Dispute evidence and review deadline

Understand the incentive design

Pure performance pricing protects the client from paying for idle capacity, while giving the provider an incentive to maximize billable units. The written definition must make quality the shortest route to payment.

A hybrid model can be healthier when research and market learning are valuable even before volume stabilizes.

Performance pricing does not remove risk. It changes which behaviors are rewarded and where disputes occur.

When the model is a good fit

Pay per qualified held meeting can fit a proven offer, a sufficiently large reachable market, clear qualification criteria, meaningful customer value and a sales team that follows up promptly.

It is usually premature when positioning changes weekly, the market is tiny, sales capacity is unavailable or the client expects the provider to guarantee closed revenue.

What does pay-per-appointment lead generation actually buy?

Pay-per-appointment lead generation is another label used for paying against a meeting-related unit. The label does not tell you whether the billable event is a booking, a held conversation or a meeting accepted against written qualification criteria. Ask for that event, the evidence and the treatment of cancellations, reschedules, duplicates and no-shows before comparing the quote.

Use the same cohort and currency when evaluating the effective cost. A rescheduled call is not automatically a new appointment, and a calendar-link click is not a booking. Beespoke’s own standard outbound offer remains a flat monthly service with no per-meeting fee; this guide helps evaluate a billing model and does not change that offer.

How to verify founder-led involvement

  • Ask who attends the strategy and weekly review calls.
  • Ask who writes and approves targeting and messages.
  • Ask who reads replies and decides what changes.
  • Ask what happens when the campaign contradicts the original hypothesis.

“Founder-led” is meaningful only when senior judgment remains connected to execution and evidence after the sale.
