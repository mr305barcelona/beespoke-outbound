What a cold-email follow-up should do

A cold-email follow-up reminds the recipient of a relevant unanswered message and makes it easy to respond. It does not need to rebuild the whole sales argument. Beespoke typically uses two email messages: an individually researched opening and one brief reminder, often about four days later. That is our usual operating choice, not a proven universal optimum or a promise of replies.

Assume the recipient can read the original email. A short “Just checking in on this” can be appropriate when the first message already explains the relevance. If the initial message is vague or aimed at the wrong person, another reminder does not fix the targeting or copy.

Choose timing from the actual send and current context

“About four days” describes a typical gap, not a compulsory calendar rule. Agree the actual delay and permitted sending window in the campaign. A weekend, timezone, absence or changed account context can affect the appropriate next action. Check the previous send and all reply channels before another message; do not calculate a follow-up from the date a list was imported.

Replies determine the next step

Stop the programmed follow-up when a reply arrives and let the responsible person review it. Interest, a question, a referral, an automatic absence message and a decline need different decisions. Do not leave a scheduled reminder running because the reply was not the positive answer you hoped for.

Coordinate channels instead of adding endless touches

Omnichannel does not mean every person receives every channel in parallel. Relevant LinkedIn activity can support a LinkedIn-first approach; where a person is inactive there and an appropriate email is available, email may come first. If there is no response after the short sequence, review whether another channel has a genuine reason and an agreed owner. Calling is a separate scoped cost, not automatically included.

Keep one conversation history so a LinkedIn reply stops a pending email reminder and the client knows what was already discussed. Channel switching should improve relevance, not bypass a rejection or hide repeated contact by different senders.

Report the conversation, not the reminder count

Separate sent messages, human replies, relevant interest, original bookings, reschedules, held meetings and qualified outcomes. Use the client’s calendar link and authorized CRM/calendar access or client confirmation to reconcile bookings. An email asking for a call is useful interest but is not yet a booked call. Preserve the original date and source when a meeting moves.

Before you hand this to a provider

Review fit, evidence and ownership together. A useful message does not establish a booking or a qualified opportunity.

  1. Approve the proposed people and exclusions.
  2. Check the source behind every specific claim and keep unknowns explicit.
  3. Agree the authorized channel, concise follow-up, reply owner and stopping conditions.
  4. Verify the calendar outcome through authorized access or client confirmation.

Frequently asked questions

How many follow-ups should we send?

There is no universal optimum. Beespoke typically uses one short email follow-up after the opening, then reviews whether another appropriate channel makes sense. Do not add touches just to fill a sequence.

Is “just checking in” too short?

It can be enough when the initial message already explains a specific relevant idea. It is not a substitute for a good first message and does not establish a response-rate advantage.

Should email always come before LinkedIn?

No. Choose from the person’s relevant activity and the agreed campaign scope. An active LinkedIn user may be better approached there first. Keep channel ownership coordinated and respect replies and declines.

What if someone replies before the reminder?

Stop the programmed follow-up and review the response. Interest, questions, referrals, automatic replies and declines need distinct handling; do not continue merely because it was not a positive reply.
