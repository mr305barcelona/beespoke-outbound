Choosing the work to outsource

Outsourced SDR or lead generation agency: which model fits?

An outsourced SDR service and a lead generation agency can describe the same provider. Choose staffed capacity when the missing resource is a person or team with a verified allocation and management arrangement. Choose managed execution when you need an agreed programme run for you. Compare who researches, decides, supervises, responds and hands work to sales; the label alone tells you none of that.

Compare the work, not the category name

An agency may employ dedicated SDRs, share specialists across campaigns or supply research alone. An outsourced SDR may work under the provider’s manager, the buyer’s manager or a shared arrangement. “Dedicated” needs a written allocation; “managed” needs a named delivery owner. Neither word establishes coverage or results.

SDR outsourcing and staff augmentation

Three complete decisions, including “do not hire yet”

Buyer A: a closer is ready, but nobody can run prospecting

A fictional B2B stock-planning adviser has sold a defined service through manual introductions. The intended buyers are operations leaders at US distributors. Existing customers, previous declines and companies outside the serviceable market are excluded. The founder can judge the list, approve factual boundaries and take discovery calls, but cannot supervise daily research, execution and replies.

A managed email/LinkedIn programme is a plausible arrangement because execution and supervision are the missing work. Research-only support leaves that work unowned; a staffing-only proposal does too unless management is explicitly included. The model choice is conditional: provider capacity, permitted sender access and handoff evidence are still missing.

Decision: hold the proposed managed arrangement for those artifacts, not for a meeting promise. The buyer still owns list judgment, discovery and closing. No campaign is authorised by this teaching decision.

Buyer B: specific coverage inside an existing sales operation

A fictional technical supplier has a tested buyer group, product training, an internal sales manager and a CRM. It needs an assigned representative during four specified weekday mornings in the buyer’s calendar time zone, with participation in the team’s review rhythm. The requirement is coverage and integration, not simply another channel campaign.

Staffed SDR capacity is worth evaluating if the proposed allocation, supervisor, training and absence arrangements satisfy that brief. A managed agency could also provide it; the agency label does not rule it out. A campaign promising only a monthly output does not establish the required availability.

Decision: choose the capacity arrangement for diligence, pending written allocation and allowed access. Ask separately whether the required channel and language are included. Neither native calling coverage nor an immediate replacement is assumed.

Buyer C: the offer and call owner are unresolved

A fictional founder asks for more meetings but cannot name the buyer, defend the offer or identify who will respond and run discovery. There is no evidence of a repeatable sale yet. Both an assigned rep and a managed campaign would inherit unresolved decisions.

Decision: defer full execution. Clarify the offer, buyer and follow-up ownership through founder discovery or a separately defined diagnostic task. Outside research can support learning, but that is a different purchase from a predictable meeting programme. No launch date or result is invented.

Change one constraint in Buyer A: if a capable internal manager and execution time become available, research-only support plus internal execution becomes a credible alternative. The offer and buyer have not changed; the missing capability has. That is why a model should not win by default.

Read three fictional proposals against Buyer A’s brief

Scope R — research only

“We deliver a researched account list with inclusion reasons and approved source fields. Your team runs outreach and replies.”

The output is clear, but Buyer A has no execution owner. Reject this arrangement for the current brief, rather than calling research-only work a poor service. It could fit the changed-constraint situation.

Scope M — managed email/LinkedIn

“A delivery owner manages research, approved messages, authorised outreach, replies and calendar handoff. You approve the target list and factual boundaries, then run the sales calls.”

This covers the missing work. Hold until the proposal specifies allocated capacity, permissions, channels, visibility and the evidence handed to the closer. “Managed” alone does not answer those questions.

Scope S — assigned representative, unclear supervision

“An assigned SDR works in your CRM. You supply product training. Management is shared.”

“Assigned” does not establish dedicated hours. “Shared management” does not identify who coaches, resolves a reply exception or covers an absence. Request an allocation and responsibility map before judging this scope.

One fictional opening, not a reusable claim of results

Your question about repeated follow-ups after the distributor event made me wonder how your team routes replies today. We help teams keep the handoff clear between research and sales. If that is a current task, would a short outline be useful?

  • Known context: a relevant role and an explicit public question in the invented situation.
  • Reasonable question: whether the team is working on reply routing now.
  • Unsupported claim: that the company is losing revenue or urgently needs an agency.
  • Human judgment: refer to the actual point rather than add an empty compliment or an “I noticed” opening.

A location, event, shared interest or social post can create a genuine reason to write without proving a business problem. Evaluate research judgment, not how many personalization fields appear. The copywriting and account-based prospecting guides own the fuller examples.

Inspect the handoff and the exception

An interested reply is not yet a qualified meeting. In the fictional M01 record, the contact accepts a discussion but purchasing authority is initially unknown. The booking is created on day 3; the sales owner receives the question, known fit, open issues, calendar/time zone and original booking reference. The call is held on day 12; only then does the client confirm the agreed fit and accept an opportunity.

Ask for evidence without exposing a customer or prospect

Request an authorised redacted sample that explains the period, purchased work, original cohort, qualification definitions, client’s retained work and material limitations. Remove identities and reidentifying context, not only names. If permission is missing, use an original fictional demonstration and say that it is not result evidence.

  • Method: how a record moves from eligible account to outreach, reply and sales handoff.
  • Measurement: original booking, cutoff and held/qualified/opportunity decisions kept separate.
  • Limits: what is missing, pending or not attributable.
  • Permission: what can be shown, to whom, and in what form.

A logo, review score or total meeting count does not establish what this buyer will receive. Do not copy another provider’s customer outcomes into Beespoke proof or contact references as part of reading this guide. The agency-selection guide carries the fuller evidence-review process.

Questions

What is the difference between an outsourced SDR and a lead generation agency?

The labels overlap. An agency can provide SDRs, and an outsourced SDR service can include management, research and execution. Compare the allocated capacity, supervision, channels, systems, deliverables and client responsibilities in the written scope.

Does outsourced SDR mean a dedicated representative?

No. Allocation may be dedicated, shared or defined around a particular programme. Verify the actual time or coverage, supervisor, included work and absence arrangements; the label does not establish availability.

When is a managed campaign a better fit?

It may fit when the offer and buyer are clear, the client can judge the list and take sales calls, but execution and supervision are missing. Verify the provider’s scope and capacity. If the offer or follow-up owner is unresolved, defer full execution.

Who handles discovery and closing?

Under Beespoke’s approved scope, the client runs discovery, proposals and closing. Beespoke manages the agreed prospecting work and calendar handoff. A booked call is separate from a held call, qualification, client-accepted opportunity or sale.

Are calling and dedicated SDR staffing included in Beespoke’s base plan?

The base outbound plan covers cold email and/or cold LinkedIn. Calling is quoted separately according to the target market, with native callers hired for that work. Dedicated full-time SDR staffing is not automatically included.

How long should the evaluation take?

Use review points tied to preparation, launch, responses and a sufficiently mature sales cohort within the actual contract. Do not assume a universal ramp or time to meetings. Beespoke’s approved outbound minimum is three months.

How should costs and results be compared?

Use the same scope, exact cost period, cohort and observation cutoff. Separate original bookings, held/qualified meetings and accepted opportunities. Missing costs remain unknown; cost per qualified meeting is undefined when there are no qualifying held meetings.
