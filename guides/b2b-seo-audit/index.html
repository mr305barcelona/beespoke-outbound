B2B search diagnosis

B2B SEO audit: find and prioritize what blocks qualified demand

A B2B SEO audit checks whether search engines can discover the right pages, whether those pages answer real buying questions, and whether visitors can progress toward a relevant conversation. Combine technical checks, page-level search evidence, content quality and source-aware conversion measurement. The deliverable is a prioritized work queue with evidence, an owner and a verification date—not an unexplained health score.

Start with the business question and a page inventory

Write down the offer, buying roles, markets and languages before opening a crawler. A page can attract many visits from people who will never buy your service. Agree which buyer questions the website should answer and what a useful next step looks like.

Inventory canonical public URLs, their purpose, language, linked service and most recent substantive change. Keep service pages, evaluation guides, pricing explanations and customer evidence as separate cohorts. Compare equivalent dates and search surfaces; annotate launches, migrations and changes to tracking. A newly published page needs discovery and time before its performance can be judged.

Check discovery, indexability and the selected canonical

Sample important templates and every business-critical URL. Inspect the response status, redirects, robots rules, meta robots, canonical, sitemap inclusion and internal links. Search Console can distinguish discovery, crawling and indexing; an accepted sitemap submission establishes none of the later stages by itself.

Compare the declared canonical with Google’s selected canonical where that information is available. A real duplicate, blocked response or inaccessible stylesheet calls for a specific repair. “Discovered, not indexed” calls for investigation of quality, duplication, linking and crawl evidence; it does not automatically justify deleting the page.

  • Check that the final HTTPS URL loads useful HTML and required assets.
  • Test reciprocal language alternatives, self-referencing canonicals and relevant links within each language.
  • Record the affected URL, observed response, date and reproduction steps. Retest the same failure after repair.

Assign buyer questions before changing titles or content

Use page-filtered Search Console queries to see what Google currently associates with a URL. Record impressions, clicks, CTR and position with the date range, country, device and row coverage. Position is an average across different searches; it is not a single stable rank. Missing query rows are unknown, not proof that nobody searched.

For a page losing clicks, separate lost impressions from changes in CTR. Check which queries and countries changed, whether the average position moved and whether the results page now serves a different intent. A broader impression mix can reduce aggregate CTR without making every existing query worse. Compare the same query/page cohort before rewriting a successful answer.

Give each important question a primary page. Improve the title, concise answer, evidence or internal links only where the observed search intent supports it. Similar keywords do not always need new URLs; a genuinely different buying job sometimes does.

Review content, proof and the path to a decision

Read the page as a potential buyer: does it answer the question quickly, explain limits and help you decide? Check factual accuracy, current service scope, dated sources, concrete examples, responsibilities and the next sensible action. A long article that avoids the decision can still be incomplete.

Inspect actual competing results for the question, noting their format and useful decision help. Add what the buyer needs that your page lacks; do not copy their claims, fabricate credentials or expand into unrelated subtopics merely to increase word count. Review the whole translated journey, including tools, pricing links and booking handoff.

Test mobile usability and genuine lead measurement

Check navigation, readable headings, tables, contact controls and the booking path on desktop and narrow screens. Diagnose measured loading or interaction problems on representative templates before changing shared styles. Browser zoom and accessibility settings belong to visitors; production CSS should not compensate for a tester’s changed zoom.

Keep impressions, organic landing sessions, calendar opens, genuine new bookings, calls held and qualified outcomes separate. A confirmation-page visit alone does not prove a booking. Reconcile booking identifiers and original creation dates with the scheduling system where permission and data permit.

Exclude identified internal and audit visits before interpreting traffic growth. Preserve unknown attribution rather than classifying all Direct traffic as testing. When the cohort or source coverage is incomplete, report the limitation instead of calculating a confident conversion forecast.

Prioritize an evidence-backed work queue

Fix confirmed access or booking failures on important pages before cosmetic changes. Next, address demonstrated intent gaps and internal journeys. Treat uncertain explanations as tests with a baseline, owner, expected observation and review date. Estimate effort and dependencies separately from potential buyer value.

Know what a useful agency audit should deliver

Ask for a reproducible URL-level issue register, the data period, access limitations and a clear distinction between confirmed failures and hypotheses. Each recommendation should name the proposed change, responsible person and how success will be checked. A checklist export without interpretation leaves prioritization with you.

An audit does not establish that implementation is included. Confirm who changes code or content, who approves commercial claims, and who validates the live release. If you need ongoing implementation, discuss that scope separately. No responsible audit can guarantee a number-one position or a fixed number of meetings.

A reproducible technical audit: observation, alternative, repair and retest

Choose an important URL and record the exact test before expanding to the whole template. The same symptom can have different causes. An error on your connection is not proof of a universal outage; a declared canonical is not proof that Google selected it. Keep the raw evidence reference and access limitation in the register.

In URL Inspection, Google-selected canonical belongs to the indexed data. A live test cannot predict which version Google will select as canonical. Record the exact evidence type instead of treating a successful live fetch as canonical or indexing proof.

Separate a changing query mix from a failing answer

For this fictional guide, compare the same country, device and complete 28-day windows. Query A moves from 100 impressions and 5 clicks to 100 impressions and 5 clicks: 5% CTR in both. Query B moves from 0 impressions and 0 clicks to 400 impressions and 0 clicks. The combined total changes from 100/5 to 500/5, so CTR falls from 5% to 1% while clicks stay at five. That arithmetic does not prove that the existing answer deteriorated.

Inspect Query B’s actual results: does it ask for a calculator, service, definition or a different buying stage? If it belongs here, improve the missing answer. If another existing URL owns it, improve the internal relationship. If disclosed query rows do not reconcile with page totals, state the coverage gap instead of inventing an explanation for the remainder.

For an actual click decline, compare impressions and CTR within the stable named-query cohort, then inspect country, device, position and result-format changes. Annotate the release; observe mature results after the change. A concurrent booking or ranking movement is not a causal experiment.

Audit authority and corroboration without chasing a score

Inspect relevant referring pages, their actual destination, whether the link is live and whether it helps a buyer corroborate a claim. Compare lost links with changed destinations, redirects and removed source pages. Vendor authority metrics can help prioritize investigation but do not verify a penalty, expertise or ranking benefit.

Check whether public company facts, named expertise, permitted case evidence and editorial sources agree. A real gap may be an accurate reference that points to a retired URL; the first fix is a working destination. Do not buy links, invent reviews, contact publishers or submit a disavow file from an unexplained backlink spike.

From diagnosis to implementation acceptance

Ask the agency to show one completed issue before accepting a long report: affected URL, dated observation, alternative explanation, buyer impact, proposed repair, dependency, owner and retest. Confirm the access needed, the scope of the sample and which data was unavailable.

Agree who can edit templates, who approves facts and who validates the release. Acceptance needs the response or behavior that failed to pass under documented conditions, plus preservation of useful URLs, languages, tracking and buyer paths. The later business review asks a different question: did mature relevant search exposure and genuine qualified conversations improve?

Choose the next fix by evidence and buyer impact

Leave with a short work queue, not a large unranked export. Preserve working URLs and recent improvements while testing the specific constraint.

Frequently asked questions

What should a B2B SEO audit include?

A defined buyer and page inventory, crawl and index checks, page/query evidence, content and proof review, internal journeys, mobile usability and source-aware lead measurement. Its output should be a prioritized action register with owners and verification steps.

Is an SEO audit the same as a content audit?

They overlap. An SEO audit diagnoses search discovery and performance across technical and content factors. A content audit decides what the existing portfolio should keep, improve, combine or retire and where a new page is justified.

How often should we repeat the audit?

Review critical access and measurement after meaningful releases. Revisit the wider diagnosis after substantial site changes or when sufficiently mature page/query evidence changes. Use comparable periods rather than repeatedly reacting to daily noise.

Will fixing every issue guarantee higher rankings?

No. Fixes can remove barriers and improve usefulness, but demand, competition, search presentation and evaluation change. Record what changed and assess mature query-level exposure and genuine business outcomes.
