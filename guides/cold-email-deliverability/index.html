Cold email deliverability: diagnose the problem before sending more

Cold email deliverability is the ability of prospecting emails to reach a recipient’s inbox. A sending platform’s “delivered” status usually establishes server acceptance, not inbox placement or human interest. Diagnose the actual symptom, recipient provider and sending cohort before changing copy, buying domains or increasing volume. Authentication is necessary in the applicable circumstances; it cannot guarantee that unsolicited mail is wanted or reaches the inbox.

SMTP 4xx responses indicate temporary negative completion; 5xx indicates permanent failure for that request. Preserve the full provider text and stage. A fictional receiver returning 451 during message transfer has not accepted that message. A 550 at RCPT TO needs its full reason before an address is marked invalid. A 250 after completed DATA means acceptance of responsibility, not inbox placement; a 250 at an earlier stage does not prove acceptance of the whole message. Record the actual provider, timestamp and responsible technical role beside the response.

Open tracking is not a dependable inbox-placement measure. Privacy protections, automated activity and blocked images change what it records. Google says it does not track opens or verify third-party open-rate accuracy. Use human replies as evidence of engagement, without treating silence as proof of filtering.

A provider’s allowed account quota is not a universally safe cold-outreach volume. Rules for personal Gmail or Outlook.com consumer destinations are not a description of every business tenant’s inbound policy.

For qualifying Gmail bulk marketing or promotional mail, one-click unsubscribe requires the appropriate RFC 8058 headers; a body mailto link or preferences page is not a substitute. Google also requires a visible body unsubscribe link. Honor requests promptly, maintain suppression across sequences, and check the applicable provider requirements rather than assuming one mechanism covers every case.

Microsoft’s updated April 2025 post retains older junk-first timeline text alongside its revised rejection notice. Do not read either as a new 2026 deadline. Inspect the actual provider response. Meeting authentication rules does not establish recipient permission, legal compliance or guaranteed placement.

A record can exist while the actual message fails alignment

Ask the authorized mailbox or DNS administrator to inspect a message received through the actual sending path. A settings screenshot or a public record lookup is useful, but neither proves the result of that message. The receiving server’s Authentication-Results header supplies more specific evidence.

Here SPF and DKIM pass for delivery.example, but neither authenticated identity aligns with outreach.example in the visible From field. A pass beside each mechanism is not enough. This simplified example does not include every header or forwarding condition; it is not a DNS record to paste into a production account.

An authorized technical owner should check the legitimate sending sources, active signing configuration and alignment using the mailbox provider’s instructions. Moving DMARC to p=reject on a calendar schedule can disrupt legitimate mail if sources are not ready. Conversely, p=none is not a promise that a failing message will be delivered normally.

A technical pass cannot make unwanted outreach useful

  • Check the reason for contacting each person, data context and exclusions. An address verifier can reduce uncertainty about reachability; it cannot verify interest, permission or every catch-all recipient.
  • Apply opt-outs and agreed suppression at send time across accounts and sequences. Do not switch channels or domains to get around a decline.
  • Review real identity, accurate subjects, relevant personal or professional context, links and unnecessary tracking. A public post, shared interest or location can be relevant when true; inventing familiarity cannot.
  • Separate provider quotas from operating limits. No fixed number of emails per day, domain age or warm-up period guarantees safety. Gradual legitimate sending and actual feedback matter; artificial engagement scores are not recipient demand.
  • Operational separation can reduce some risks, but domains, subdomains and sending paths may share signals. New infrastructure or a dedicated IP is not an automatic fix. Avoid rotation to sidestep enforcement.
  • Do not treat spintax, a rigid word limit or a list of forbidden words as a technical cure. Read the message as a person would, and investigate actual errors before guessing at filters.

Applicable privacy, marketing law and sending-service policies need their own review. Publicly listed or freshly scraped information is not permission by itself. This guide explains delivery evidence, not a legal approval to send.

Questions about deliverability

Why are messages accepted but not getting replies?

Acceptance does not establish inbox placement. Placement tests and provider evidence can help, but weak targeting, timing or an unconvincing offer can also explain silence. Compare cohorts before choosing a fix.

How many cold emails per day are safe?

There is no universal safe count. Provider quotas, sender classification, sending history, audience and actual errors are different inputs. A fixed daily ceiling cannot guarantee placement or permission.

Why is Google Postmaster Tools empty?

Check verified access, personal Gmail traffic, volume/privacy limits and reporting availability. Missing data is unknown, not a zero complaint rate or a good reputation grade.

Will warm-up or a new domain fix the problem?

Neither proves recovery. Fix the underlying configuration or sending/recipient problem and evaluate real evidence. Artificial engagement and rotating domains do not establish that prospects want the messages.

Does every hard bounce mean an invalid address?

No. Permanent responses can concern recipient addresses, policy or authentication. Record the SMTP stage and full provider response, then involve the appropriate technical owner.

When can we resume?

After the relevant cause is addressed, evidence and suppression are reviewed, and a named owner defines a controlled restart, monitoring and rollback conditions. No calendar interval or score is sufficient by itself.
