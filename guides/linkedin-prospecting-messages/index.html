Start with a conversation you could actually have

An operations contact has accepted a no-note invitation. A public company announcement describes a second office. The IT provider, contract and support arrangements are unknown. The sender is a fictional IT services firm that genuinely covers both locations.

Fictional training examples. People, circumstances and outcomes are invented. These are not client messages or evidence of campaign results.

First substantive DM · after acceptance

Hi [first name], With the second office opening in [area], are you the person who coordinates IT support across both sites? We help teams in that area cover the day-to-day support between offices. I’m asking so I don’t send the wrong person a longer explanation.

Possible reply · not a scheduled step

I coordinate it. We already have a provider—what would you handle?

Answer the question first

Thanks. Our work is the everyday support coverage between the two offices, alongside an existing provider where that division is useful. If your current arrangement already covers that, there may be nothing to change. Is there a part of the two-site coverage you are reviewing?

The sender must replace that explanation with its real scope. A website announcement does not establish dissatisfaction with the current provider. The answer leaves room for the prospect to say that everything is covered.

Alternative later reply · only if the person asks

That is worth discussing. Can you send your calendar?

Requested handoff

Of course—here is our calendar: [approved client calendar link]. I’ll pass on the question about two-site coverage so the person taking the call has the context.

  • The first DM has one primary question, supported by an honest explanation of the sender’s work.
  • A human reply stops a programmed no-reply reminder. The next message answers what was actually asked.
  • A requested calendar link is a handoff. Only a separate calendar record or authorized client confirmation establishes a booking.

If there is no reply instead, review the context and send at most the brief reminder in the timing example when appropriate. Do not schedule the scope answer or calendar handoff as if the prospect had already responded.

Choose the relationship before the wording

Check the actual relationship, available controls and any existing conversation. A public profile is not evidence of frequent inbox use. Relevant recent activity may support LinkedIn as the first channel; a suitable business email may be more appropriate when that activity is absent.

A genuine shared event: invitation, then a distinct DM

In this fictional situation, both people attended the same public session. The sender genuinely works on the topic. They have not spoken privately, so the note must not say that they met.

Invitation note

Hi [name], we both attended [session]. Your question about practical manager training stayed with me. I work on that too and would be glad to connect.

After acceptance

Thanks for connecting, [name]. At [session], your question was about helping new managers practise difficult conversations. We run workshops around those situations. Is that something your team is currently looking at, or was it a more general question?

Possible reply

A general question for now. We are not looking for a provider.

Respect the actual answer

Understood—thanks for clarifying. I’ll leave it there.

The note and DM have different jobs. An accepted request does not justify every later message. The last reply closes this commercial sequence; it is not a reason to start an email pitch.

An InMail to a non-connection: a truthful subject and a small request

A fictional design studio has checked a public product release and the relevant product owner. An appropriate InMail route is available. Whether the release created a new user journey remains unknown.

It is a new role. Do you have an example?

Fulfil the request

Yes—[example link] shows how we map one role’s first-use journey, with the assumptions marked. The example is illustrative, not a result from your product. If useful, which part of that new role’s journey are you reviewing?

The reason does not have to be company news

A reason to write may come from a public personal post, a genuine shared interest, a location or an event. It should be professionally appropriate and something you can explain without making the person feel watched. Common ground can start a conversation without proving any buying need.

Fictional connection note

Hi [name], I’m based in [city] as well. Your post about making local professional events more welcoming raised a good point. I’d be glad to connect.

Being in the same city is a genuine connection in this scenario. It does not establish a local office, friendship or need for the sender’s services. Do not use a neighbourhood detail simply to demonstrate how much you found.

A public interest, without forcing a sales angle

Fictional DM · already connected

Your point about keeping beginners involved in [public community activity] made me rethink how we introduce people at our own events. Which part of the format has made the biggest difference for you?

This question is appropriate only if the sender genuinely cares about that answer and the stated experience is true. It can remain a conversation. Do not manufacture a hobby, pretend that a casual exchange is a sales qualification or pivot immediately to a calendar link.

Keep the trust behind an introduction or offer

A shared connection is not an endorsement

A visible shared contact may never have spoken to the prospect. Before using a name as an introduction, ask about the actual relationship and obtain permission. If the person only identifies the right role, describe that accurately.

Do you know [person] well enough to introduce us? I would like to ask about [specific topic] because we work on [relevant area]. If you are comfortable, I can send two lines you could forward. No problem if you do not know them well.

Only after a real, permitted introduction

Hi [first name], [mutual contact] introduced us about [topic]. We help [relevant type of team] with [specific work]. Is that an area you are reviewing, or have I misunderstood the timing?

They ask what you handle

Possible incoming reply

We already have someone doing this. What would your part be?

Response to adapt

We would cover everyday support across the two offices and agree which issues still go to your existing provider. If that coverage already works well, there may be nothing to replace. Is there a part of the site coverage you are currently reviewing?

Pause the programmed reminder. Confirm the scope with the responsible client if it is outside the approved explanation.

They request details or a resource

Possible incoming reply

Can you send the details here?

Response to adapt

Of course. The workshop lets new supervisors practise a difficult management conversation using a situation from their work, with feedback on the approach. It would complement their induction rather than replace the whole programme.

Deliver what was requested. Do not treat the request as permission to send a calendar link instead.

Response to adapt

Thanks for pointing me in the right direction. Is there a role I should look for, or would you prefer I leave it there?

Edit the whole message, not a list of forbidden words

AI-assisted and human-written drafts need the same review: supported facts, individual relevance, a truthful commercial purpose and a proportionate ask. A natural message may mention a post the sender actually read. The problem is empty framing or invented familiarity, not proof that a particular word came from AI.

Generic praise becomes a specific question

Before · fictional editing exercise

Hi [name], I read your comment on [public topic] and was impressed by your remarkable journey. We leverage innovative solutions to unlock growth. I would love to connect, send our deck and arrange a call.

After · same commercial purpose, clearer reason

Your comment on [public topic] raised a question about [specific part]. Is that something you handle at [company]? We work on that area. I wanted to check the owner before sending a longer explanation.

The revision removes unsupported praise and competing asks. It keeps the sender’s actual work and one reason to reply. Only use the public-topic reference after checking what was really said.

A long no-reply pitch becomes a short reminder

Before · only appropriate to assess as a draft

I know you are incredibly busy, but I wanted to follow up again on our transformative solution and all the ways it could help your team. Have you seen my message, looked at our deck or found a time for a call?

After · if unanswered and still appropriate

Hi [first name], just checking whether the question above is relevant to you.

The reminder depends on a useful original. If the original was unsupported or badly targeted, a shorter follow-up does not fix it. A real reply needs an actual answer rather than this reminder.

    Questions about LinkedIn prospecting messages

    Should we use one LinkedIn template for everyone?

    Use a structure, not identical relevance. Check the person, role and available route, then rewrite the substantive reason for that recipient. A name and company substitution is not enough.

    Does an accepted connection count as a lead?

    It is a connection state, not evidence of buying interest, qualification or a booking. Record the actual conversation and verified outcomes separately.

    What if the person is inactive on LinkedIn?

    Review whether an appropriate business email or another authorized route is more suitable. A profile existing does not establish that the person reads LinkedIn messages regularly.

    Are these real client messages?

    No. The worked conversations and tool samples are fictional training material. They illustrate the founder-described research and writing approach without publishing client, sender or prospect identities or claiming results.

    How long should a LinkedIn message be?

    Use the space needed for a clear, relevant point and one primary ask. Invitation notes have different constraints from DMs and InMail. The linked help lists 200 characters for personalized notes; check the current product and composer rather than copying a limit from another product.

    How many follow-ups should we send?

    There is no universal optimum. Beespoke typically uses an individual substantive opening and one brief reminder, often about four days later, subject to the actual context. A reply, decline, timing request or unavailable route changes the decision.

    When can we count a meeting as booked?

    After an authorized calendar/CRM record or client confirmation verifies the booking. A link send, accepted request, reaction or page view does not prove it. Keep original bookings, reschedules, held meetings and qualified meetings separate.
