Account research and outreach

Account-based prospecting: from account fit to a useful sales conversation

Account-based prospecting starts with a company-level reason for fit, then identifies the people and questions that could justify a conversation. Research is not evidence of buying intent. Choose the depth your team can support

Start with the offer, customer economics and an identifiable buying problem. Account-based prospecting suits situations where a narrower set of organizations has plausible need and the team can research them meaningfully. If the offer is unclear or the sales owner cannot follow up, more precise lists will not solve the underlying constraint.

Distinguish prospecting from the wider account-based marketing programme. Prospecting concerns selecting accounts, opening relevant conversations and handing them over. Broader ABM can involve advertising, content, customer expansion and several teams. Do not assume that buying an outreach service includes all of that work.

Choose light research when the offer is clear and the buying group is simple. Use deeper research for a specific multi-role question that justifies the extra work. Hold when fit, ownership or reply capacity is unresolved. There is no universal contract value or account count that makes this method appropriate.

Start with two completed account briefs

These accounts and signals are invented training examples, not companies researched by Beespoke. Account A is a fictional industrial testing business that has announced a second site. The hypothesis is that a multi-site operating change could create a relevant coordination question. Expansion does not prove a problem, budget or intention to buy.

Account B is a fictional software vendor that appears relevant by industry but publicly describes selling only to individual consumers. Our hypothetical campaign serves firms selling to business procurement teams. This contradictory evidence is a reason to hold or exclude B, not to force it into a target list.

Test the ICP against counter-evidence

Define which organization could buy, why the problem could matter and what public evidence would support or contradict the hypothesis. Separate customer accounts from referral partners, distributors and suppliers. A company can be useful to the business without being a buyer of the same offer.

Set exclusions before collecting names: existing customers, active opportunities, unsuitable sectors, conflicts and organizations outside the delivery scope. Record the source and date of each useful signal. A public expansion announcement may support research, but it does not prove budget, urgency or permission to make a stronger claim.

Use your own won, lost, retained and poor-fit customer patterns where you have permission and sufficient evidence. Compare the buying problem, delivery fit and commercial outcome—not just familiar logos. With no history, label the ICP a hypothesis and test a small cohort; never invent customer evidence.

Identify the account before merging records

Give each account a stable ID and record the legal or operating entity, parent, division, location and buying unit separately. Two contacts sharing a domain may belong to different buying units; two domains may belong to one organization. A matching domain is a clue to review, not permission to merge or contact again.

For fictional A, record A as the company and A-site-2 as a location, not a second acquired account. Before merging an import, the list owner checks the entity, shared conversation, opportunity and exclusions. Record whether a refusal applies to one person, a unit or the whole account. Keep uncertain scope on hold; do not route around a restriction through a sibling record.

Allocate effort, do not predict buying

Create a small number of working tiers based on relevance and evidence quality. Higher priority means a stronger reason to research or contact, not a promise that the company will buy. Missing information should remain unverified; a familiar logo is not a buying signal.

Map a useful question to each role

The same title can have different responsibilities in different firms. Map the problem owner, commercial decision, operational or technical reviewer and possible champion as hypotheses. Do not label someone “decision-maker” merely because a data provider supplies a senior title. A small firm may combine roles; a larger one may add procurement or legal review.

Find a human reason to write

The reason can come from the person: a relevant public post, an event question or genuine shared local context. Use it only when it connects honestly to the conversation. Sharing a city does not prove operational pain; a personal post does not justify inferring health, finances, politics or private circumstances.

State the fact directly, then ask a modest question. “Your question about cross-site handovers…” is more specific than empty praise. “I saw” and “I noticed” are not forbidden; swapping them mechanically does not make a message human. Individual reasoning and a truthful offer matter more than a supposed AI-word detector.

Choose the first channel from the evidence

Recent relevant LinkedIn activity can make LinkedIn a sensible first channel. An inactive profile with an appropriate, permitted email route may point to email. Neither activity nor a discovered address guarantees permission or a reply. Check campaign and suppression rules. Calling is a separate scope and market decision.

Our usual practice is one contextual opening and a brief follow-up around four days later, adjusted to context—not a universal optimum. For the email above: “Hi [First name], just checking whether the handover question is relevant. Happy to send the outline if useful.” Avoid repeating the pitch at length. Before changing channel, review the shared record: silence is not new interest, and a decline must not restart elsewhere.

Finish the supplier-and-next-month example

For Account A, an operations question could be: “Your fictional second-site announcement raised a question about who coordinates the handoff between locations. Is that part of your remit?” A technical question would instead ask which requirements matter if a change is considered. Neither should pretend that a breakdown, budget or active project is known. Coordinate the account approach so multiple people do not receive unrelated contradictory claims.

Fictional reply: “I coordinate that work, but we already have a supplier. I could discuss the handoff requirements next month.” Record confirmed remit, existing supplier and suggested timing exactly. This is a relevant reply, not proof of dissatisfaction or a qualified opportunity. Ask only the agreed necessary qualification questions and offer a useful next step.

A reviewable handoff includes account and role, public evidence reference/date, actual confirmed need, exact source of permission or interest, existing supplier constraint, unknown budget/authority, agreed next step, booking identity if genuinely created and the owner of follow-up. The client then owns discovery, proposals and closing. Preserve a decline or exclusion so another list import does not restart unwanted contact.

A useful clarification: “Thanks. Would it help to map the handover requirements without assuming a supplier change? If so, what would you want that discussion to resolve, and when next month would suit?” In a fictional continuation, the contact confirms a requirements discussion in the second week, not a supplier review. Budget and purchase authority remain unknown.

Keep account and booking denominators honest

Track the selected account cohort, contacted buying roles, positive relevant replies, genuine new bookings, calls held and qualified outcomes. Define each denominator and observation window. Several contacts at one account do not become several accounts; a reschedule does not become a new acquisition.

Review which evidence and role hypotheses produced useful conversations. Do not attribute every later opportunity to the first message without source evidence. Keep outreach-created bookings separate from independently acquired website bookings even when the buyer later reads a guide. Scale only when fit, delivery capacity and commercial feedback support it.

Fictional arithmetic: 20 accounts with 40 contacts produce six replies across four accounts. That is six contact replies and four responding accounts, not six interested companies. One genuine meeting later moves to a new time: one original booking, not two. Qualification and held-call status remain unknown until recorded. These invented counts illustrate denominators; they are not expected campaign performance.

When managed outbound is the useful next step

If the bottleneck is researching and running relevant outreach, a managed service may help. If fit is unclear or sales cannot accept conversations, fix that first. The client supplies targeting judgment and approves the list and campaign boundaries; Beespoke can handle research, individual messages and programming when authorized. The client does not have to review every message.

Beespoke’s managed outbound scope focuses on cold email and/or LinkedIn, with founder-led targeting, research, messages, follow-up, replies, qualification and booking against agreed criteria. The client approves claims and exclusions and owns discovery, proposals and closing. Cold calling is a separately quoted activity according to market; it is not part of the standard email/LinkedIn scope. This does not imply a dedicated full-time SDR or guaranteed volume.

Questions about account-based prospecting
How is account-based prospecting different from a contact list?
It starts with a company-level fit hypothesis, exclusions and dated evidence, then maps buying roles and relevant messages. A contact list supplies names but does not establish a reason to contact them.
Is account-based prospecting the same as ABM?
No. Prospecting covers account selection, relevant contact, response handling and handoff. Account-based marketing can cover a wider coordinated programme involving advertising, content, expansion and other teams.
How many accounts should we target?
Use a cohort that can be researched, reviewed and followed up within the real delivery capacity. There is no universal account count. More accounts without adequate relevance or response handling can weaken the test.
Does this include cold calling or a dedicated SDR?
Those are separate scope decisions. Beespoke’s standard managed outbound focuses on cold email and/or LinkedIn. Calling is separately quoted by market; a dedicated full-time SDR is not implied.
Does a signal mean the account is ready to buy?
No. An observation can justify research or a question, not prove need, budget, urgency, authority or permission. Preserve counter-evidence and unknowns.
Should we contact several people at once?
Not by default. Keep shared account history, clear owners and role-specific reasons. A relevant reply pauses pending touches. Do not bypass a decline through another person or channel.
Does the worksheet authorize contact or verify qualification?
No. It organizes entered notes and unknowns. Responsible people must verify evidence and record actual decisions. Contact, booking, held and qualified states stay separate.
