From target market to working programme

B2B sales prospecting plan: build a campaign your team can actually run

A B2B sales prospecting plan defines which accounts and roles to approach, why your offer is relevant, what evidence and approval are required, who handles each step and how replies reach sales. It connects account research, channel choice, available capacity, qualification and review. A useful plan includes exclusions and stopping conditions; it is not a volume target or a promise of meetings.

Start with the decision the programme should support

Write the commercial learning question before the activity target. For example: can a defined buyer role recognize a relevant problem and agree that a scoping conversation would be useful? Identify the offer, market, buyer responsibilities and evidence needed to answer that question. A list of sectors is not yet a plan.

Agree what your team can actually deliver and what a suitable customer looks like. If the offer, permitted proof or ability to handle discovery is unresolved, fix that dependency before scaling outreach. A plan can legitimately conclude that a campaign is not ready to launch.

Give accounts a fit test and an exclusion rule

Record company type, operating model, market, relevant role and the reason the offer could matter. Define exclusions such as existing customers, agreed restricted accounts, conflicting business models and previous declines. Keep account and contact records distinct so an excluded company is not reintroduced through another person.

A public signal can justify research, but not an invented pain claim. Use the account-based prospecting guide for source verification and buying-role mapping. This plan then assigns how that research becomes an approved campaign, reply process and commercial handoff.

Calculate capacity from the work, not an aspirational send count

Separate research, copy review, execution, reply handling, qualification, reporting and client coordination. The limiting resource may be a reviewer or the client’s available discovery slots, rather than list size. Reserve response time before allocating every minute to new contacts.

Fictional weekly example:600 available minutes minus 180 for reply/review and 120 for coordination leaves 300 for research. At an assumed 20 minutes per account, that is capacity for 15 researched accounts. If research actually takes 30 minutes, capacity becomes 10. These are invented planning assumptions, not recommended volumes, staffing claims or performance benchmarks. More contacts per account would consume additional work.

Record the assumptions and measure actual workload in the first review. If reply or handoff work exceeds capacity, reduce new activity and agree ownership rather than leaving relevant prospects unanswered. Account capacity does not forecast replies, meetings or revenue.

Choose a coherent approach and approve it before launch

Choose a channel because it reaches the agreed roles and supports the offer responsibly, not because a tool makes it easy to automate. State sender identity, account ownership, research standards, message approval, follow-up boundaries and who can pause activity. Cold email, cold LinkedIn and calling have different dependencies; adding a channel is a scope decision.

For Beespoke, base outbound work concerns cold email and/or cold LinkedIn. Calling is separately quoted by market; required software/data costs and client responsibilities must be agreed. The client owns discovery, proposals and closing. This guide does not imply a dedicated full-time SDR team or a universal multichannel package.

Before launch, review a small research sample, the exact copy, exclusions, reply routes, booking availability and the receiving sales owner. Campaign authorization is separate from merely approving a planning document.

Work a reply all the way through to a useful sales handoff

Use explicit dispositions: wrong account, wrong person, no interest, later timing, referral, relevant question, relevant interest and unclear. A reply alone is not a buying opportunity. Preserve the actual wording needed for context, with appropriate privacy, rather than rewriting uncertainty as enthusiasm.

Fictional reply: “I oversee the workflow, but we are not replacing our current supplier. A discussion about the next site might help next quarter.” Confirmed: role, current supplier and suggested timing. Unknown: need, budget and purchasing authority. The next step is an agreed clarification or timing note, not a claim that the company wants to switch.

The sales handoff names the account/role, fit evidence, what was confirmed, what remains unknown, constraints, agreed next step and follow-up owner. If a call is genuinely booked, keep its original creation identity and later held/qualified status. A reschedule is not a second acquisition.

Measure account quality and commercial learning separately

Keep accounts researched, contacts approached, contact replies, responding accounts, relevant replies, genuine bookings, held calls and qualified outcomes distinct. Write the denominator next to every rate. If six replies come from four accounts, you have not found six interested companies.

Compare complete periods under the same definitions and annotate material offer, channel, staffing or tracking changes. Look at exclusions and reasons for poor-fit responses, not only positive counts. A small test can reveal a clear workflow failure without supporting a precise expected conversion rate.

A website-assisted visit from your outbound campaign does not automatically become a website-acquired lead. Keep origin evidence so outreach performance and organic website performance are not confused.

Before you act

A useful plan ends with named owners and a reviewable launch decision. If fit, proof, approvals or receiving sales capacity are missing, resolve those dependencies first.

  1. Confirm the actual buyer task and evidence.
  2. Keep the smallest justified action and explicit unknowns.
  3. Name the owner, approval and useful next check.
  4. Measure mature relevant outcomes, not activity alone.

Frequently asked questions

What belongs in a sales prospecting plan?

The offer, account and role criteria, exclusions, evidence, channel scope, capacity, owners, approvals, reply dispositions, qualification, handoff and review. It should state what prevents launch and what triggers a pause.

How many accounts should the plan contain?

There is no universal number. Estimate available research, review and reply capacity, then test the assumptions. A large list does not create capacity or prove buyer fit.

How is this different from account-based prospecting?

Account-based prospecting focuses on selecting and understanding accounts and buying roles. This plan owns the end-to-end programme, responsibilities, capacity, launch controls, replies and sales handoff.

Does this workbook execute outreach or save inputs?

No. It creates a local working register and optional CSV or print output. It sends no inputs, saves none after reload and does not contact anyone.

Can the plan guarantee a number of meetings?

No. It makes assumptions and responsibilities explicit. Actual response, booking, attendance and qualification depend on the offer, market, execution and sales process and must be measured separately.
