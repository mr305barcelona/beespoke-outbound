# Beespoke SEO growth plan: authority, citations, next cluster, and site audit

Updated: July 21, 2026

## Executive decision

The next growth constraint is authority and distribution, not page count. The site has 80 public URLs, no broken internal links, no orphan pages, canonical and hreflang coverage, responsive section navigation, reading progress, repeated contextual CTAs, and a submitted sitemap. The next 90 days should favor relevant third-party proof and links, client-supported evidence, one deliberately linkable original asset, and a small buyer-intent content cluster.

Do not buy bulk guest-post packages, submit to hundreds of generic directories, exchange sitewide links, publish fake reviews, or create domains solely to link to Beespoke. Those links are low-trust and can create manual-cleanup work without producing qualified demand.

### Priority 2: links based on real commercial relationships

These are likely to outperform directory links because they are editorially relevant and supported by actual work.

1. Ask each client represented in a public case study whether its website can link to the corresponding evidence page from a partner, supplier, testimonial, or results page.
2. Offer each participating client a concise, pre-approved case summary and neutral attribution. Never reveal a client that has not authorized public attribution.
3. Ask software/data vendors genuinely used in delivery for a customer story, expert quote, integration story, or agency-directory profile.
4. Ask Barcelona founder communities, accelerators, coworking communities, and international-business groups where Noah is genuinely a member for a member profile—not a paid link insertion.
5. Turn podcast appearances, webinars, conference panels, or expert interviews into links to the most relevant guide or case study rather than always linking to the homepage.

Suggested destinations:

- Cybersecurity clients/partners → `/case-studies/cybersecurity-linkedin-lead-generation/`
- Media/PR relationships → `/case-studies/media-partnership-outreach/`
- Sales and founder communities → `/guides/outbound-lead-generation-cost/`
- LinkedIn/tool partners → `/services/linkedin-lead-generation/`
- Barcelona/Catalonia communities → `/ca/` or the future Barcelona service page

### Priority 3: earn links with an original asset

Build one cite-worthy data asset before publishing another broad guide:

**2026 B2B Outbound Pricing and Qualification Benchmark**

- Collect 25–40 publicly disclosed agency price points with retrieval date, service scope, billing unit, channel mix, and whether meetings are booked or held.
- Publish the normalized dataset as an accessible HTML table plus downloadable CSV.
- Include a transparent methodology, correction form, update history, and definitions.
- Create one embeddable chart with a required source attribution link.
- Pitch it to sales newsletters, RevOps publications, founder communities, agency comparison writers, and vendors whose pricing is included.

This is substantially more linkable than another generic “tips” article and extends the site's existing pricing authority.

- Review GSC links, referral sessions, qualified calls, and assisted conversions. Keep sources that send relevant visits; stop work on profiles that produce neither trust nor traffic.

## Internal-link audit

Current findings:

- 80 HTML pages found.
- Zero broken internal page links.
- Zero orphan pages.
- Every language variant links to all four versions.
- The lowest-prominence English pages have four internal inbound links: the agency-vs-freelancer comparison and three industry pages.
- The homepage exposes eight buyer resources but not the full library.
- The SEO template has global links to Services, Case Studies, Pricing, the homepage, author, three related resources, and contextual in-article resources.

Recommended changes:

1. Create `/resources/` as a browsable hub grouped into Services, Pricing & Economics, Comparisons, Industries, Case Studies, and About/Editorial.
2. Add `Resources` to the desktop navigation and footer of every SEO page and homepage.
3. Add an “Industry solutions” block to the service pages so consulting, SaaS, cybersecurity, manufacturing, and future professional-services pages receive contextual links from the strongest commercial nodes.
4. Add a “Recommended next step” link at the end of every informational guide, chosen by intent rather than by a generic related-card rotation.
5. Give new cluster pages at least three contextual inbound links at launch: one service page, one adjacent guide, and the resources hub.
6. Keep localized links language-consistent. Never send a Spanish article's primary next step to an English page when a reviewed Spanish equivalent exists.

## UX, UI, accessibility, performance, and international audit

### What is working

- Clear typography and visual hierarchy.
- Desktop sticky article guide and mobile sticky section picker.
- Reading-progress indicator and active-section state.
- Responsive tables, calculators, checklists, related cards, and CTA blocks.
- Repeated CTAs without turning every paragraph into a sales pitch.
- Mobile-aware navigation, reduced-motion support, descriptive page titles, canonical URLs, hreflang, schema, and favicon coverage.
- Homepage now links directly into major commercial and evidence pages.

### P0/P1 issues

1. **Localized homepage editorial quality:** translation QA catches defined forbidden terms, but some phrasing remains literal or inconsistent. A native-language editorial pass is required for all three homepages and the highest-impression localized pages before expanding language inventory.
2. **No central content hub:** users and crawlers cannot browse the full library from a single prominent location.
3. **Social preview asset missing:** pages define Open Graph title/description but no branded `og:image` or `twitter:image`, weakening shared-link presentation and outreach.
4. **Business entity consistency:** there is no documented canonical fact sheet for name, address/service area, phone, founding date, logo, short description, and social profiles. This must exist before citations are created.

### P2 issues and fixes

1. Homepage image elements lacked intrinsic dimensions and lazy decoding. This release adds width/height, lazy loading, and asynchronous decoding to below-fold logos and the founder photo.
2. Keyboard focus visibility was inconsistent. This release adds a sitewide high-contrast `:focus-visible` treatment.
3. The 1080×1080 founder PNG is approximately 1.5 MB. Create an optimized WebP/AVIF derivative and a responsive `srcset`; keep the PNG only as a fallback.
4. Add a keyboard-visible “Skip to main content” link to both templates.
5. Add a branded 1200×630 social-sharing image and explicit image dimensions/alt metadata.
6. Add automated checks for missing image dimensions, relative localized assets, duplicate IDs, missing main landmarks, empty links, and broken section anchors.
7. Run monthly real-user Core Web Vitals review in Search Console; synthetic Lighthouse scores alone are not a substitute for field data.

## Measurement

Track every placement in one sheet with:

- Domain and exact profile/article URL
- Opportunity type and topical relevance
- Target Beespoke page
- Contact, submission date, status, and follow-up date
- Live link, `rel` attribute if visible, and anchor text
- Referral visits, engaged sessions, calls, and qualified opportunities
- GSC appearance date and linked-page query changes

Success after 90 days is not “50 backlinks.” A credible target is 8–15 live, relevant referring domains; 3–5 genuine client reviews; one original asset that earns editorial citations; measurable referral visits; and upward movement for the linked commercial pages.
