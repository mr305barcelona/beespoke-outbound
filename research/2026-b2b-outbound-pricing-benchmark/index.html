What the published prices actually show

Public pricing spans software-like account capacity, shared appointment setting, managed single-channel campaigns, multilingual SDR capacity, multichannel programs and full team structures. Treating all of them as one agency average would create a misleading number.

The useful finding is not a universal market price. It is that buyers must normalize delivery model, included infrastructure, output definition, variable fees and commitment before a retainer becomes comparable.

Which offers are genuinely comparable

Start by grouping offers into software or AI-assisted capacity, managed campaign services, outsourced SDR capacity, hybrid performance models and system-build or training engagements. Compare within a group before comparing across groups.

Even inside one group, identify the channels, sender or seat count, list and data work, technical infrastructure, reply handling, qualification, meeting attendance rule and client management required.

A $500 platform subscription and a $5,000 human-managed campaign may both produce meetings, but they transfer very different work, risk and accountability.

Build the fully loaded buyer cost

Add the monthly fee, amortized setup, software, data, sending infrastructure, variable outcome fees and the client's management time. Then calculate cost per qualified meeting held and per opportunity accepted by sales over a meaningful learning period.

If a provider reports leads or bookings, do not silently treat them as attended ICP meetings. Keep booked, held, qualified and accepted-opportunity events separate.

  • Base retainer or seat fee
  • Setup fee divided across the planned term
  • Per-lead or per-meeting charges
  • Required software, data and profile costs
  • Internal review, reply and sales-management time
  • Replacement, ramp and cancellation exposure

Qualification changes the economics

A low headline price is expensive when meetings miss the written company, role, geography, interest or attendance rules. A higher price can be rational when it replaces substantial research, infrastructure and management—provided the resulting conversations can progress.

Before launch, define the event that triggers billing and the event that sales accepts. Those definitions should appear in the contract and reporting, not remain implied in sales copy.

Methodology and limitations

Beespoke collected prices displayed on provider-controlled web pages and checked each source on July 24, 2026. The dataset records the provider's wording for the price and then classifies billing unit, model, channels, output and commitment for comparison.

No currency conversion is applied, because exchange rates change and false precision would weaken the comparison. Provider performance claims are recorded only as scope or output descriptions; they are not independently validated. Custom-price offers without a numeric public price are excluded.

  • Primary provider pages only
  • One row per disclosed offer or channel-specific price
  • Native currency preserved
  • Setup and variable fees retained in the price field
  • Beespoke rows clearly marked as publisher offers
  • No claim that the sample represents every provider or final negotiated price
Disclosure: Beespoke publishes this research and sells outbound services. Its two offers are included for completeness and are identified in every dataset view.

Use the benchmark without misreading it

Normalization checks before comparing two offers
FieldComparable only whenCommon mistake
Delivery modelBoth transfer a similar amount of execution and managementComparing software with a human-managed team
Billing eventLead, booked meeting, held meeting and accepted opportunity are defined the same wayCalling every positive reply a meeting
Channel scopeSender count, channels and infrastructure are alignedIgnoring domains, data, profiles or dialer costs
CommitmentSetup and minimum term are included in the evaluation periodComparing month one against a mature monthly run rate
Client workReply handling, sales follow-up and management burden are accounted forTreating internal time as free

A defensible buying sequence

  1. Shortlist offers by delivery model and channel fit.
  2. Normalize fixed, setup, variable and required third-party costs.
  3. Write the qualified-and-held meeting definition.
  4. Model pessimistic, expected and optimistic opportunity progression.
  5. Choose the smallest test that can produce a credible learning signal.
