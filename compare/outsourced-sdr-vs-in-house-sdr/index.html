Hiring or outsourcing an SDR

Outsourced SDR or in-house SDR: which model should you choose?

Build in-house when the work is repeatable, you need lasting internal capability and someone can coach the SDR. Outsource a defined job when the provider’s actual capacity and methods fit it, while keeping a client owner for commercial decisions and sales follow-through. Compare the same decision calendar, the work included, cash commitments and management time. Then compare equivalent active periods; a salary and an agency retainer do not automatically buy the same job.

Compare the work, not just the employer

An outsourced SDR can mean an assigned representative, a shared team or a managed campaign. A remote hire can still be directed by your own sales manager. Confirm who decides daily priorities, writes messages, supplies data, reviews quality and handles replies before treating two prices as equivalent.

Neither model guarantees meetings, instant product knowledge or uninterrupted coverage. Ask for the specific operating plan rather than assuming that “dedicated,” “managed” or “remote” defines it.

What problem are you trying to solve?

If the segment or message is unproven, bound the learning task before buying permanent capacity. Either an internal operator or an external provider can run a well-defined test.

If execution capacity is missing, compare actual availability, assigned hours and retained client work. The employment label alone does not prove speed.

If the motion is repeatable and strategically important, weigh internal coaching and learning against the external scope and handover. Keep long-term capability visible alongside short-term cost.

Put hiring and outsourcing on the same 12-week calendar

Start both comparisons on the day you make the decision. Track when each model is ready to approach approved accounts and when you will have enough follow-up to review the results. Recruiting, onboarding and approvals can take time even when the work is already funded. Keep those weeks in the calendar and in the cost comparison.

Try one handoff before you commit

Use the same fictional account under both models. Imagine someone replies, “I’m interested, but procurement owns this.” Ask the operator to show where they record the reply, who checks the referral and what sales will see. Keep the case open until the buyer role and other acceptance criteria can be verified. Ask for a sample export of the account rationale, approved message, reply history, suppression status and next owner. Check that another authorized team member can pick up the case from that record.

Write down what would change the decision

In this example, the team can use the external test to learn sooner if it approves the work, has time to handle escalations and can verify the handoff. It can revisit hiring when demand is steady enough to justify dedicated work and the manager can reserve coaching time. If neither model has a named owner for discovery and feedback, resolve that first. Record who will review the evidence, when the decision comes due and what would cause the team to change course.

Cost the first 12 weeks from the decision date

Extend the fictional calendar above with explicit assumptions. The hire starts in week five; first approved outreach remains week seven. The external test is billed from week one and begins approved outreach in week three if ready. Use three four-week planning periods, not an assumed calendar quarter. None of these inputs is a salary benchmark, provider quote or Beespoke price.

For the hire, assume 4,500 USD planned pay plus 900 USD employer costs per four-week period, paid for two periods, tools at 300 USD for two periods and a 2,000 USD recruiting invoice. Management uses the existing three-hour weekly allowance at an assumed 60 USD per hour. For the external test, assume 4,000 USD per period for three periods, tools at 300 USD for three periods, 1,000 USD setup and 1.5 client hours weekly at the same hourly value.

Test the operator and the available manager time

A hire needs more than a job description, and a provider needs more than a sales presentation. Use the same fictional or sanitized brief for the actual person who would do the work. Include only approved product facts, account-fit criteria and the required channels.

Ask for a reason to contact a particular buyer, a channel choice, one opening and a brief follow-up. Then correct one fact and ask for a revision. Look for accurate reasoning, natural language, restraint and the ability to apply feedback—not a polished pitch by someone who will not operate the campaign.

Name who answers product questions, approves target lists, handles difficult replies and owns discovery and closing. Reserve real weekly time and absence cover. Outsourcing can move execution management; it does not remove your commercial responsibilities. If nobody can own the sales handoff and feedback, resolve that before choosing either model.

Check continuity before you need it

Keep account rationale, approved messages, replies, qualification decisions, suppression records and next actions available to the authorized team. Clarify permitted use of licensed data; paying a fee does not automatically grant unrestricted ownership or reuse.

Run the export and handoff test in the worked example before depending on either an employee or a provider. Another authorized person should be able to identify the next action without reconstructing the conversation from memory.

Choose for readiness and durable demand

Hiring can fit when the motion is proven, there is sustained work for the role, the manager can coach it and direct product learning matters. An external program can fit a defined test or capacity gap if its people, start date and retained client work have been verified. A bounded test can also run with existing internal staff.

A hybrid needs a real division of responsibility. For example, keep internal ownership of an established segment and use external execution for a distinct test. Share suppression decisions and define who owns each account so two teams do not contact the same person independently.

Review both the initial plan and a steady-state twelve-month plan using actual commitments and expected capacity. The 12-week illustration above does not end employment obligations or establish a supplier’s cancellation rights. External retainers can be fixed during a minimum term, and internal capability can have value that a short-term cost table does not capture.

  • Choose in-house for verified ongoing work and available coaching.
  • Choose external execution for a defined job the provider can actually cover.
  • Choose a hybrid only when responsibilities and account ownership are clear.
  • Delay expansion when the audience, offer, sales owner or operating capacity remains unresolved.

Frequently asked questions

Is an outsourced SDR cheaper than an in-house SDR?

Not automatically. Compare the same job, dates and commitment, including tools, employer costs, setup, recruiting and retained management. Separate invoices from valued internal time. An outsourced retainer can remain fixed during its minimum term.

When should a company hire an in-house SDR?

When the work is sufficiently repeatable and durable, product learning or control matters, and a manager can support the role. Verify the audience, available work, coaching and sales handoff rather than using a universal revenue or meeting threshold.

When does outsourcing fit better?

It can fit a bounded test, verified capacity gap or specialist job when the provider’s actual people and scope match the work. Product onboarding, approvals and client sales follow-through still matter. Faster launch is a plan to verify, not a guarantee.

Does Beespoke place a full-time SDR?

No. Beespoke provides a founder-led managed campaign for cold email and/or cold LinkedIn, not full-time SDR staff augmentation. The base service is USD 1,500 monthly, or EUR 1,500 in Europe, with a three-month minimum; calling and agreed third-party extras are separate.

How should ramp time enter the cost comparison?

Use the actual hire date and paid periods. Salary during onboarding is already part of the pay line; do not add it again as a second salary cost. Track delayed activity separately and compare equivalent active periods after comparable follow-up.

Can we combine an internal SDR with an external provider?

Yes, if account ownership, responsibilities, suppression records and reporting are coordinated. Agree who changes targeting and messages, who handles replies and what can be handed over. Two overlapping teams with separate records do not automatically form one program.

Which results should we compare?

Track original bookings, attendance, qualification, sales acceptance and wins separately. Keep the audience, work, active period and follow-up comparable. A rescheduled booking is not automatically another new booking; missing qualification is unknown, and zero qualified held meetings does not mean zero spend.
